# PLFS monthly and quarterly bulletins (MoSPI), April 2025 to March 2026

All figures are Current Weekly Status (CWS), age 15 and above, all-India, in per cent, copied from MoSPI tables. Tables were extracted with `pdftotext -layout` and parsed by script, then checked against every other release that repeats the same month or quarter, and against the chart values in the March 2026 monthly press note and the January to March 2026 quarterly press note. Nothing here is interpolated or estimated.

"PDF p" means the page number in the PDF file. "pr. p" means the page number printed in the bulletin footer.

Months marked in the swing table are where the maximum or minimum occurs. Ties are all listed.

**How the swing compares with sampling error.** The WPR RSEs in MB-Nov25 Statement 5 (PDF pp13-14, July to November 2025) are 0.35-0.37% for rural male, 0.86-0.95% for rural female, 0.44-0.49% for urban male and 1.33-1.38% for urban female. That makes one standard error about 0.27, 0.33, 0.33 and 0.32 percentage points (pp) respectively; this is my arithmetic, not a MoSPI figure. The rural swings, 1.9 pp for males and 5.0 pp for females, are far larger than that. The urban swings, 0.9 pp each, are about what sampling noise alone would produce over 12 months. Rural female WPR falls to a low in June 2025 (33.6), climbs steadily to a high in December 2025 (38.6) and eases in March 2026 (37.3). April 2026 (36.5) is close to April 2025 (36.8), so the movement looks seasonal rather than trend.

### Monthly breakdowns of persons outside the labour force, and of status in employment

**Not published.** None of the monthly bulletins (MB-Apr25, MB-Oct25, MB-Nov25, MB-Apr26) or monthly press notes read (May, June, August, September, October, November and December 2025; January, February and March 2026) has a table of persons outside the labour force (students, domestic duties) or of workers by status in employment or industry. The quarterly bulletins do not have a not-in-labour-force breakdown either. The only monthly figures of this kind are in the narrative text of two press notes, and neither is tabulated nor broken down by all four groups:
- PN-May25, p3 of 8: in rural areas, the share of employment in the primary sector (agriculture) fell from 45.9% in April to 43.5% in May 2025. The note also says some unpaid helpers moved to domestic chores.
- PN-Jun25, p3 of 8: the share of rural female workers in agriculture fell from 70.2% in May to 69.8% in June 2025. The note also says "several unpaid helpers in June 2025 reported themselves as engaged in domestic duties", and that the share of own-account workers rose in rural areas from May to June. No number is given for either.

## Part 2: quarterly bulletins, April-June 2025 to January-March 2026

Each quarter is taken from its own bulletin: Apr-Jun 2025 from QB-AJ25, Jul-Sep 2025 from QB-JS25, Oct-Dec 2025 from QB-OD25. \* Self-employed (total) also includes people with self-employment work who did not work in the reference week because of sickness or other reasons. That is why it can exceed own-account worker plus contributing family worker. Contributing family worker is the old "unpaid helper in household enterprise", a rename only (QB-AJ25 footnote 4, PDF p8). Self-employed, regular wage/salaried and casual labour add up to 100.

### Section 3: rotational panel design

Every selected household is visited four times in four consecutive months: a first visit, then three revisits. The rotation runs over a two-year panel covering 2025 and 2026. Each month's estimate uses all four panels in the field that month (25% first visits, 75% revisits), so 75% of first-stage units (FSUs) are matched between consecutive months (PN-May25 Annexure II, p7 of 8). Each quarterly estimate uses only two panel-visits per month. For Oct-Dec 2025, for example, these are P17RV3 and P18RV2 in October, P19RV2 and P110RV1 in November, and P111RV1 and P112FV in December. This gives 50% FSU matching between consecutive quarters (QB-OD25 section 3.1, PDF pp14-15; same wording in QB-AJ25 and QB-AJ26). **MoSPI publishes no flows, transition matrices or other matched-panel or longitudinal figures** in any monthly or quarterly bulletin read here. The panel is described only to explain how samples overlap and which visits feed each estimate.

Because a quarterly estimate uses half the monthly sample, it is not the average of its three months, though the two are close. Rural female WPR is 35.4 in the Apr-Jun 2025 quarter against a mean of 35.2 for the three months, and 37.6 in Jan-Mar 2026 against 37.9.

## Breaks and definitional changes

1. **January 2025 redesign.** The PLFS sample design changed from January 2025: new FSU selection, basic stratum and stratification, FSU households raised from 8 to 12, and a new rotational panel. The survey schedule changed too. MoSPI says the results are not directly comparable with PLFS releases up to December 2024 (MB-Nov25 section G, PDF p15; QB-AJ25 section 5, PDF p15).
2. **Coverage changes.** Quarterly bulletins up to December 2024 covered urban areas only. Apr-Jun 2025 is the first to cover rural and urban areas. The monthly series starts in April 2025. There are no published monthly or rural-quarterly figures for January-March 2025.
3. **Terminology.** "Contributing family worker" replaces the old "unpaid helper", with the same definition.
4. **No break inside April 2025 to March 2026.** There are no revisions (see the check under Sources), and the definitions and table layouts stay the same across the window. The tables only widened from one month (April 2025 bulletin) to rolling five-month statements.
5. **Minor inconsistencies inside the bulletins.** The text of QB-AJ25 (para 2.5.2, PDF p11) says 70.9% of rural female workers were in agriculture, and 60.6% and 64.9% of urban male and female workers were in the tertiary sector. Statement 4 on the same page gives 71.0, 60.7 and 64.8; I used the Statement values. Separately, the RSEs in MB-Apr25 Statement 5 (for example 0.02% for rural male LFPR) are about ten times smaller than those in later bulletins (0.33% for July 2025), which looks like a different scale. I have not used them.
6. **Absolute numbers.** The worker counts in the quarterly bulletins (section 2.3) use MoHFW population projections. I did not extract them.
